# Executive Summary

**Headline.** Over January 2024 – December 2025 the marketing programme invested €53,110 across 120 campaigns and five channels, reaching 2,032 customers and generating 804 conversions worth €194,352. That is a blended ROAS of 3.7x and a customer acquisition cost of €66 — a healthy return. On average 11.3% of reached customers converted, with an average order value of €242. Year-on-year, attributed revenue declined 19% in 2025 versus 2024.

**What worked.** Email was by far the most efficient channel: ROAS 6.5x and CAC €31, despite receiving only 6% of spend. Loyal customers responded at 18.7% (vs. 3.2% for At-Risk) and delivered ROAS 7.3x, confirming that the existing CRM base is the cheapest source of revenue. Seasonality is a real asset: November–December months returned 1.4x the revenue of an average month.

**What did not work.** Display consumed €3,660 and returned €3,070 (ROAS 0.84x, CAC €159) — i.e. every euro spent returned less than a euro. Across all campaigns we also measured clear diminishing returns: the within-channel elasticity of ROAS to spend-per-customer is -0.71, meaning that doubling the intensity of a campaign lowers its efficiency by roughly 39%. Several of the largest campaigns are operating beyond the efficient frontier.

**Customer intelligence.** K-means RFM segmentation of 566 buyers isolated a 'Champions' group of 50 customers (9% of buyers) who generate 24% of buyer revenue (avg €950 each), while 379 'One-off Lapsed' buyers purchased once and have not returned. A Random Forest response model (hold-out ROC-AUC 0.75) ranks customers by propensity: targeting only the top 30% of scored customers would capture 62% of all responders, a 3.2x lift in the top decile. Key drivers are spend per reached customer (negative: diminishing returns), Search channel, Loyal segment. A churn model (CV AUC 0.68) shows that 76% of 2024 buyers did not purchase again in 2025; the strongest retention signals are number of campaign touches, Loyal segment, days since last purchase.

**Recommendations.** (1) Re-allocate 10–15% of budget from Display and the largest low-ROAS Social flights into Email lifecycle programmes and mid-sized Search campaigns; (2) cap spend-per-customer per campaign and split large flights into smaller targeted waves to stay on the efficient side of the diminishing-returns curve; (3) front-load Q4 budget and run a summer 'always-on' minimum instead of heavy July–August pushes; (4) operationalise the propensity score in the CRM so that campaigns only contact the top three deciles, and launch a win-back journey for 'One-off Lapsed' and high-churn-risk customers; (5) track ROAS, CAC and Response Rate weekly in the Power BI dashboard against a 2.5x ROAS target.
